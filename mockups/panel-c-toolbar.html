Demand forecasting, explained.

How eighteen months of sales history becomes a decision you can act on – and why most forecasts fail before the model is even chosen.

Every operations team runs on a forecast, whether it admits it or not. The spreadsheet that says "order 400 units" is a model; it is just a model nobody maintains. This note walks through what changes when you treat forecasting as a product rather than a chore, using the same pipeline described in our demand playbook.

Why forecasts drift

Forecasts rarely fail loudly. They drift – a few percent a month – as promotions, seasonality, and supplier delays reshape the signal underneath a model trained on last year's world. The cost shows up elsewhere: stockouts on the ten products that matter, capital parked in the two hundred that don't.

From data to decisions. Faster.

A minimal pipeline

The working core is small. History in, calibrated intervals out, one job that reruns weekly:

python
model = QuanticaForecaster(horizon=12)
model.fit(sales["2024-01":"2026-06"])  # eighteen months of history
forecast = model.predict(ci=0.9)
forecast.to_dashboard("demand-q3")
